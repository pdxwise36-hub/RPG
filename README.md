# Emberfall

A small turn-based RPG for your phone, built as an installable web app (PWA) — no app store, no build step.

## Play it

Serve the folder over HTTP and open it on your phone (or in a desktop browser at a narrow width):

```
python3 -m http.server 8765
```

Then visit `http://<your-machine-ip>:8765/` from your phone on the same network, or `http://localhost:8765/` locally. On mobile, use "Add to Home Screen" to install it like a native app (works offline after the first load).

## How to play

- Move with the on-screen D-pad or arrow keys.
- **Town is completely separate from the levels, and completely safe** — its layout is fixed (same every playthrough, never regenerates) and has no grass tiles at all, so no random encounter can ever happen there. It's your permanent home base: Town Center (rest for free, General Store, Armory, Bounty Board, and — once you've beaten the true ending — New Game+), the Knight, the Master Mage, the Pet Tamer, the Arena, the Boss Rush gate, the Rival, and Deckard Cain all sit at fixed spots you'll come to know by heart. An exit portal at the south end leads out to the levels.
- Walking through grass in a level risks a random encounter; the path is safe. Battles are turn-based: Attack, cast a Skill (costs MP), use an Item, or Run.
- **Town Scrolls** teleport you straight back to Town from anywhere in the levels — buy them at the General Store or find them in chests. Walking into Town's exit portal opens the **World Map** — every zone in chain order, each showing whether you've reached it, its boss status, and your Bestiary progress there. Reached zones are one-tap travel; unreached ones show as locked instead of a guessing game about what's still ahead.
- Winning fights grants gold and XP; leveling up raises your stats and fully heals you. The XP needed per level compounds through level 10 (the original curve), then grows by a flat amount per level after that so grinding through all 14 levels stays achievable instead of demanding an ever-exploding amount of XP. Regular (non-boss) wins also have a chance to drop a treasure chest — gold, a Potion/Ether/Greater Potion/Greater Ether/Town Scroll, an **Unidentified** weapon, armor, helmet, gloves, or boots, or a scroll that teaches a random skill you don't already know for free. Both the gold and the gear tier scale up the deeper you've traveled, so chests found late in the run are worth noticeably more. Unidentified gear doesn't reveal what it is (or become equippable) until you bring it to **Deckard Cain** in Town, who identifies it for a small fee.
- The Greater Potion and Greater Ether (bought at the General Store, or found in chests) restore more HP/MP than their basic counterparts for a steeper price — every consumable, old or new, shows up automatically in the Shop, the Status screen, and the battle Item menu.
- The Knight and the Master Mage each teach 12 permanent skills for gold through a **skill tree**: two 6-skill branches per vendor (Knight: Guardian's Path / Warlord's Path; Mage: Frostfire Path / Stormcaller's Path), each a straight chain where a skill is locked until you know the previous one in that same branch AND have reached its tier's character level — so you choose how to spend your gold across the two paths instead of just buying down one fixed list. Physical techniques and spells respectively, though mechanically both just spend MP for bonus damage.
- **Elemental effectiveness**: every Skill (and every zone) carries an element — Fire, Ice, Nature, or neutral Physical/Void. Fire beats Nature, Nature beats Ice, and Ice beats Fire, in a small rock-paper-scissors triangle; casting the right Skill against a zone's element lands "Super effective!" for extra damage, the wrong one "Not very effective..." for less. Resolved against whichever zone you're physically standing in, so Arena/Boss Rush/Rival fights (fought from Town, which carries no element) stay neutral — it's the exploration-and-boss loop where matchups matter. Skills carrying a real element show a `[Fire]`/`[Ice]`/`[Nature]` tag wherever they're listed.
- The Pet Tamer sells ten battle pets, cheap-and-weak to rare-and-strong: Turtle, Wolf Pup, Fox, Hawk, Boar, Salamander, Owl, Baby Golem, Panther, and the Dragonling. **A full party of up to 5 companions fights together** — every party member lands its own bonus hit on the enemy each round, not just one, and every one of them earns the exact same XP from a kill (no level cap, same curve as your own character, so none of them lag behind). Slot 0 is the **Leader** (the one shown on the main battle sprite, and the one Rally/Fusion use); the rest are **Support** members shown as smaller icons alongside it. Manage the roster from the Pet Tamer's Your Companions list — **Add to Party** (up to 5), **Remove**, or **Make Leader** to promote a support member to the front slot — switching is always free. Abilities add up across the whole active party too: two Guardians reduce more damage than one, two Blessed companions find more bonus gold, and so on — a bigger, more varied team is a real, compounding upgrade, not just more raw damage. A pet's level and XP stick with it even if you bench it, and the Status screen's dedicated **Party** tab gives every member its own card — level, per-hit damage, ability (locked or unlocked), Held Item, Fusion bonus, and its own XP bar — plus Companion Charm and Mercenary info, all kept off the main Status tab so that one stays a clean, pet-free summary of your own character.
- The party Leader's normal auto-hit each round also charges a small **Companion Skill** energy meter shown on the battle screen (fed by every party member's hits, not just the Leader's own); once full, a dedicated button spends it on one big Rally burst from the Leader in place of that round's usual attacks — a real "save it up or use it now" choice. Each companion ability flavors its own Rally with a bonus effect once it's unlocked (Lv. 10): Vampiric heals double, Guardian shields your next hit, Berserker hits harder, Swift guarantees your next dodge, Blessed finds bonus gold on a kill.
- A **Held Item** (chest-only, never sold) sticks to one specific companion permanently — even benched, unlike a Charm which only helps whichever pet is currently active. Six exist: Lucky Egg (+XP), Power Band (+that companion's own power), Focus Sash (+your crit), Leftovers (heals you % HP/turn), Golden Bell (+gold), Quick Claw (+your dodge) — assign one to whichever companion is active from the Pet Tamer's Held Items section; giving it to a new companion clears the old holder, since only one companion can hold a given item at a time.
- A hireable **Mercenary** (Pet Tamer's Mercenary Camp section) is a second ally distinct from your Pet roster — no XP or leveling, just an upgrade path (Rookie → Veteran → Elite → Champion, pay the difference to advance) plus its own small Weapon/Armor loadout bought at the same section. Auto-attacks every round alongside your companion, and its Armor converts into a modest, capped damage reduction on hits you take instead of doing nothing. Once hired, it also picks one ability from the exact same five a Companion learns (Vampiric, Guardian, Berserker, Swift, Blessed) — free to swap any time, no gold cost — stacking with a Companion who's picked the same one, so a Merc and a pet both running Guardian reduce more damage than either alone.
- **Every regular monster in the game can be captured, and owning one never blocks catching another** — buy a Capture Orb (or the stronger Greater Capture Orb) from the General Store, or find either in a chest. The **Master Capture Orb** is a tier above Greater again and is never sold at any price — the only way to ever own one is finding it in a chest, with rising odds the deeper you go. Pick which orb type you want to use by default from the Status screen's Items section (tap **Select**); that choice then gets its own dedicated **Capture** button right on the main battle menu — one tap instead of digging through the Item submenu. Catch chance rises the lower the enemy's HP, topping out around 70-95% on a nearly-dead target (Master starts from a much higher base, so it's strong even against a healthy target); every catch is its own permanent companion instance (its own level, XP, and Held Item), so a second catch of a species you already have is never wasted — especially since a monster caught while **Elite** keeps a permanent power bonus of its own (stacking with Shiny on the rare catch that rolls both), making a repeat catch genuinely worth chasing. Capturing swaps out the usual kill rewards for a new companion instead of gold/XP, and bosses can never be captured — the boss-gated level chain is untouched. The Pet Tamer's **Your Companions** list groups your roster by species — tap one to open every individual copy you own (each with its own level, Shiny/Elite badges, and party controls) instead of cramming every copy into one flat list; **Adopt a Pet** is always available too, even for a species you already own. A **Browse** row above the list carries two cycling buttons — **Sort** (Species Power, Name, Most Owned, or Best Level) and **Filter** (All, Shiny Only, Elite Only, Locked Only, or In Party Only) — so a roster with dozens of duplicate catches stays easy to scan instead of always being one long fixed list. The Bestiary and Status screen both track how many of the realm's monsters you've caught.
- **Companions evolve, and learn an ability.** Any companion — bought or caught — learns a passive ability at level 10 (Vampiric life-steal, Guardian damage reduction, Berserker crit chance, Swift dodge chance, or Blessed gold/XP) that applies while it's your active pet, then evolves at level 15 into a stronger form (a flat damage boost, a name change, and a golden glow on its sprite). The Status screen's **Abilities** tab spells out exactly what each of the five does at its real numeric value, plus which Tamer pets carry it, instead of leaving you to guess from the name alone. Any companion also has a small chance to be **Shiny** the moment you first acquire it — permanent, a further power bonus, and a distinct cyan shimmer (stacking with the Evolved glow if it's both). A **Companion Charm** — found only in chests, never sold, ten tiers strong — boosts whichever companion you currently have active; equip whichever you've found from the Tamer's own Companion Charm section, or straight from the Status screen without a trip to Town.
- **Fusion** (its own entry in the Pet Tamer) is a dedicated two-step picker: choose a **Base** (the companion that keeps fighting) from your whole roster, then any number of other companions to sacrifice as **Fusion Material**, all at once — works across species and across duplicate copies of the same one (bought or caught, mixed freely). Every species row in **Your Companions** also carries its own **Fuse** button (defaulting to that species' best copy as the Base) and every individual instance in its Manage view has one too, both jumping straight to picking the Material — no need to hunt for the separate Fusion entry first. Material candidates are ticked with a **Select** toggle instead of fusing immediately, so dumping a whole pile of low-level duplicates into your main companion is one tap per copy and a single **Fuse Selected** confirmation, not a separate pick-and-confirm loop repeated per copy. Ordinary duplicates are listed separately from any Shiny or Elite copy (grouped under its own loud "fusing this away is permanent" warning), and the one confirmation covers everything selected — calling out by name (and count) if any of them are Shiny or Elite — so a prized catch is never lost to a misclick even in a big batch. For an even stronger guarantee, every instance's Manage view has a **Lock**/Unlock toggle — a Locked companion is hidden from the Material picker entirely (it can still be picked as a Base, since a Base is always kept, never sacrificed), so a favorite catch can never be fused away even by accident. The Base gets a flat damage bump scaling with how leveled the Material was (up to +20% per fuse, stacking with repeated fusions), plus the Material's own ability if the Base doesn't already have it — so a favorite companion can eventually carry two or more abilities at once. The Material is gone for good (though you can always catch or adopt another).
- **The Rival** (in Town) challenges you to a duel against their fixed team of three companions, back-to-back with a full heal between each, scaled to your own character level — neither of you can flee or capture the other's team. Freely repeatable, with a bonus on a full clear; a loss keeps whatever you earned along the way.
- **Set items**: nine 5-piece gear sets, each built from existing gear tiers and each dedicated to one purpose — Celestial Radiance (tier 10, makes you personally stronger: ATK/DEF/crit/dodge), Beastmaster's Regalia (tier 6, stronger companion), Sage's Vestments (tier 7, faster leveling), Fortune Hunter's Garb (tier 4, better chest-find odds), Prospector's Gear (tier 3, more gold), Battlemage's Focus (tier 2, cheaper/stronger skills), Elementalist's Attunement (tier 5, amplifies elemental effectiveness), Warlord's Vanguard (tier 8, boosts your hired Mercenary), and Warden's Bulwark (tier 9, extra DEF plus HP regen every turn). Every set is **upgradable**: 2, 3, 4, or 5 pieces equipped grants an increasingly strong cumulative bonus, so a set has value long before you've completed it, not just at 5/5. Pieces that belong to a set are tagged with its name in the Armory; the Status screen shows every set bonus currently active and how many pieces you're wearing toward it, and a dedicated **Sets** tab lists all nine sets in full — every piece needed (checked off as you equip them), and both your current and next-tier bonus — whether or not you own a single piece of them yet.
- **Legendary Items**: one fixed, permanent bonus item per equipment slot — Emberfang (Weapon, +ATK, dropped by the Molten Wyrm), The Stormguard's Legacy (Armor, +DEF, Stormguard Titan), Crown of the Deep Unseen (Helmet, +XP%, the World Serpent), The Tempest's Grip (Gloves, +Crit%, the Tempest King), and Steps of Eternity (Boots, +Gold%, the Eternal Sovereign). Each has a small chance to drop the first time you defeat its specific boss (repeatable attempts if you don't get it), reuses an existing gear tier as its identity rather than being a new instanced item, and stacks with that piece's own Set/Affix/Gem bonuses rather than replacing them — tagged by name in the Armory once earned.
- **Item quality is color-coded** everywhere a piece's name is shown (Armory, Enchant, Inventory) — a classic ARPG-style read at a glance instead of needing to check every description: plain white for a Normal piece, blue for Magic (a rolled Affix), green for a Set piece, and gold for a Legendary — whichever is rarest wins if a piece qualifies for more than one (a Legendary that's also a Set piece still shows gold).
- **Zone hazards**: the Frostreach, Volcanic Depths, and Blightmarsh each have a chance per enemy turn to deal small environmental damage on top of whatever's attacking you — frostbite, scorching heat, and toxic fumes respectively. Dodging the monster doesn't dodge the zone itself.
- A **Hall of Legacy** tab on the Status screen aggregates lifetime stats that don't reset with the daily Bounty Board — total kills, total gold earned, bosses defeated, Bestiary/companion/charm completion, and achievements unlocked — for a single "how far have I gotten" view.
- Tap the small character portrait in the top-left of the HUD to open your **Inventory** — a Diablo-style paper doll with your equipped Weapon, Armor, Helmet, Gloves, Boots, Amulet, and both Rings shown right on the character (Shield and Belt remain reserved for item types not in the game yet). A **Backpack** grid below lists everything else you own, tier-sorted with its own pixel-art icon per piece — tap any piece to swap it into its slot; a Ring instead gets two small buttons (one per ring slot) since both slots share the same pool, and equipping a ring into a slot it's already in elsewhere clears the other automatically so one physical ring can't double-count its own bonus. Helmets, Gloves, and Boots each bring a distinct mechanic instead of a flat stat: Helmets cut Skill MP cost and boost XP gained, Gloves add a chance to land a double-damage critical hit, and Boots add a chance to dodge an attack outright plus boost gold found — all shown on the Status screen alongside Attack/Defense. **Amulets and Rings are chest-only finds, never sold** — an Amulet slowly restores MP every one of your turns, and each Ring reflects a % of incoming damage back at whatever hit you, with the two Ring slots stacking.
- The Status screen (top-right of the map HUD) isn't just a summary — you can drink potions/ethers and use a Town Scroll directly from there. A **Bestiary** tab lists every monster (and boss) in every level, with a strikethrough on anything you've already defeated at least once. An **Achievements** tab tracks eighteen permanent milestones (first kill, level 10/50, every boss defeated, the true ending, all ten Tamer pets, capturing 10 wild monsters, capturing every capturable monster, finding the Celestial Charm, fusing 3 companions, clearing the Rival's full team, acquiring a Shiny companion, full Celestial gear, a complete Bestiary, Arena wave 20, 10,000 gold at once, 15 Elite kills, and the true ending on Hell difficulty) — each pays out a one-time gold reward the moment you earn it.
- Your progress autosaves (browser local storage) after town visits, battles, and area transitions — use Continue from the title screen to resume.
- The Armory sells ten tiers each of Weapons, Armor, Helmets, Gloves, and Boots (every piece with its own icon), from free starting gear up to the Celestial-tier set, always listed weak-to-strong, plus an **Enchant** section covering every one of those five slots for any piece you own — Weapon/Armor stack up to +20 ATK/DEF (10 levels, rising cost per level), while Helmet/Gloves/Boots stack a smaller top-up on whichever percent stat(s) that slot already carries — on top of the gear's own bonus, regardless of which piece is currently equipped. The battle Skill menu and the Status screen's skill list are likewise always sorted cheapest-to-strongest, no matter which order you learned them in.
- Chest-found gear has a chance to come with a bonus **Affix** — a permanent extra stat (like "of Power," "of the Fox," "of Fortune") stuck to that specific gear key forever, revealed alongside the piece when Deckard Cain identifies it. The top three tiers of every slot also carry a **Gem Socket** — the Armory's Gem Sockets section lets you slot in any owned Gem (Ruby/Sapphire/Topaz/Emerald/Diamond, each in Small/Medium/Large) for a flat bonus regardless of which of the five slots it's socketed into, and Deckard Cain will combine three of a kind into the next size up.
- **The Feral Pastures** is a true secret zone (Woolly Grazer/Stray Ram → the Shepherd) — not part of the World Map, Bestiary, or any zone-completion achievement. Unlocked (never consumed) by simply owning the Celestial tier of Charm, Amulet, and Ring all at once; Deckard Cain notices and offers to open a hidden path, after which "Enter the Feral Pastures" appears in the Town Center hub like any other farmable zone.
- Nineteen levels in a chain, each with its own monster pair and boss, escalating from the outskirts of town all the way to the true final boss: The Emberfall Outskirts (Slime/Goblin/Wolf → Dark Knight) → The Ember Depths (Bat/Specter → Lich) → The Frostreach (Frost Golem/Ice Sprite → Glacial Titan) → The Dragon's Spire (Wyrmling/Drake → Ancient Dragon) → The Sunken Ruins (Merfolk Raider/Reef Serpent → Drowned Queen) → The Whispering Woods (Thornling/Wisp Moth → Elder Ent) → The Sandscar Wastes (Dust Jackal/Sand Viper → Sand Reaver) → The Volcanic Depths (Cinder Imp/Magma Hound → Molten Wyrm) → The Shattered Peaks (Storm Harpy/Rock Wyvern → Stormguard Titan) → The Blightmarsh (Bog Leech/Plague Rat → Rotlord) → The Crystal Caverns (Crystal Stalker/Gem Ooze → Prism Colossus) → The Shadowfen (Shade Stalker/Nightmare Hound → Nightmare Drake) → The Celestial Spire (Star Wisp/Cloud Serpent → Astral Guardian) → The Void Rift (Void Spawn/Chaos Hound → the World Serpent) → The Ashen Wastes (Ash Wraith/Cinder Golem → the Ashlord) → The Storm Citadel (Thunder Hawk/Storm Elemental → the Tempest King) → The Bone Wastes (Bone Reaper/Wraith Serpent → the Bone Emperor) → The Chaos Rift (Chaos Spawn/Void Hound → the Chaos Harbinger) → The Throne of Eternity (Eternal Guardian/Timeless Wraith → the Eternal Sovereign, the true final boss).
- **Five post-game zones** extend the chain past the true ending without touching it: beating the Eternal Sovereign still shows the true-ending victory screen exactly as before, and a "Descend into the Abyss" button appears in the Town Center hub once you've earned it, opening The Abyssal Depths (Maw of the Deep/Gloomfang → the Formless King). From there the chain keeps going the normal way — beat its boss and a portal opens to The Sunless Expanse (Duskcrawler/Hollow Revenant → the Duskbound Tyrant), then The First Flame (Emberwraith/Cinderfiend → the Progenitor Ember, the very ember Emberfall is named for), then The Cinder Expanse (Smoldering Wisp/Ashborn Stalker → the Cinder Warden), and finally The Heart of Emberfall (Flareling/Emberkin → the Undying Ember, the chain's new true end) — twenty-four zones total, all farmable afterward like any other.
- A **Codex** tab on the Status screen gives every zone (main chain and post-game alike) a short lore blurb, unlocked the moment you actually set foot there — unreached zones just show "???" instead of spoiling what's ahead.
- **Elite monsters**: every regular encounter has a small chance to be a buffed "Elite" version of the same monster instead — bigger stats, better gold/XP, and a guaranteed chest on the kill. Capturing an Elite just captures the normal companion at its normal strength; the buff only ever applies to that one fight.
- **Companion Skill**: your active companion's normal auto-hit each round also charges a small energy meter shown right on the battle screen; once it's full, a dedicated Companion Skill button spends it on one big burst hit in place of the pet's usual small one for that round — a genuine "save it up or use it now" choice, not a bonus on top.
- **Titles**: a handful of the harder Achievements (Level 50, every boss, the true ending, catching every monster, full top-tier gear, a complete Bestiary, Arena wave 20) grant a cosmetic title instead of just a one-time gold payout — pick which one (if any) shows next to your name from the Achievements tab.
- **Bosses are farmable.** The boss stands on its own permanent tile and never disappears — walk up to it any time for a rematch, first kill or fiftieth, for full gold/XP every time. The portal onward sits on a separate tile right beside the boss instead of the boss tile itself turning into one; it's sealed (walking into it just tells you to beat the boss first) until you win that fight once, and then it's open for good, so you can freely refarm the boss afterward without ever losing access to the next level. Every level's own entrance portal leads straight back to Town regardless of how deep you are — it never chains through intermediate levels or "closes," so walking back is always a one-step trip.
- Every level is procedurally generated — the winding path from entrance to boss and the scatter of trees/water are randomized fresh each time you step into that level, so it's never the same straight corridor twice.
- The map's grass, path, water, and trees are no longer flat color fills — each now carries real per-tile texture (grass blades, worn dirt flecks, rippling water, a grounded tree canopy with its own shadow and highlight), varied tile-to-tile via a deterministic hash so a long stretch of the same terrain doesn't read as one repeating stamp. Battles got their own pass too: landing or taking a hit flashes the sprite and pops a floating damage/heal number, and a real hit on you shakes your own portrait — all computed from whatever each round's HP actually changed by, so it works uniformly for a plain Attack, a Skill, a companion's own hit, or the enemy's counter-attack alike.
- **The Arena** (in Town, mirroring the Pet Tamer) is an endless wave-based challenge for when you want something to do besides pushing through levels: each win pits you against a tougher reskinned-and-scaled version of a regular monster (drawing from progressively deeper zones as the wave count climbs) for better-than-normal gold and XP, and you choose after every win whether to press on for wave N+1 or bank your rewards and leave. Your progress is saved — losing or leaving only costs you the in-progress attempt, not the climb itself; next time you fight, you pick back up just past your best cleared wave (shown in the Status screen) instead of re-grinding from wave 1. Pushing past a genuinely new personal-best wave (not a repeat clear after a loss) also pays a bonus chest every 10th wave, with a shot at a random unowned Legendary item thrown in every 25th — a reward for the endless ladder itself, on top of the wave's own gold and XP.
- **Boss Rush** (in Town) fights every boss you've ever beaten, back-to-back, with a full heal between each — an endurance tour rather than a grind, paying one big bonus only on a full clear. A loss ends the run with no bonus, but you keep whatever each individual boss along the way already paid out.
- **The Bounty Board** (inside the Town Center hub) offers three objectives that reroll every real-world day — defeat N enemies, earn N gold, clear N Arena waves, or win N boss fights — each claimable once for a gold reward.
- **The Caravan** (also inside the Town Center hub) offers a single item that rerolls every real-world day, drawn from things that are otherwise chest-only — a Charm, a Held Item, a Gem, or (rarely) the Master Capture Orb — for gold, purchasable once per day. Priced well above what any of these are actually worth in expected chest-farming gold, so hunting chests stays the smart way to get one; the Caravan is just a guaranteed (if pricey) alternative when you want a specific day's find right now.
- **New Game+** unlocks once you've beaten the true final boss: every level's progress resets and you walk the chain again from the outskirts, but your character (level, gear, pets, skills, gold, and all collection progress) carries over untouched, while every enemy and boss scales up further (and pays out more) each cycle. Start it from the victory screen the moment you beat the true ending, or any time after from the Town Center hub.
- **Difficulty** (Town Center hub) is a separate axis from New Game+, Diablo-style: Normal → Nightmare → Hell, each tougher tier unlocked by beating the true final boss on the one before it. Nightmare and Hell don't just hit harder — every enemy/boss/Arena wave/Boss Rush/Rival fight scales up in stats *and* in gold/XP payout (by different amounts, so the harder tier is always the more rewarding one too), and it stacks independently with New Game+ if you want both at once. Beating the true ending on Hell earns its own "Hell Conqueror" achievement and title.

## Project layout

- `index.html`, `css/style.css` — screens and mobile-first styling
- `js/data.js` — stats, enemies, items, equipment, and the `MAPS` registry: Town (fixed, no boss/enemyPool) plus nineteen procedurally-generated monster levels chained purely by `nextMap`, and one further post-game-only zone (the Abyssal Depths) reached via a Town button instead of the chain
- `js/mapgen.js` — `generateZoneGrid()` builds a randomized winding path from entry to boss for the 19 levels (with a reachability check/retry so one's never accidentally unsolvable); `getTownLayout()` returns Town's one fixed, hand-authored grid (including the Arena tile)
- `js/state.js`, `js/save.js` — game state, per-zone layout caching, `currentLevelId` tracking (which level Town's exit / a Town Scroll returns you to), and localStorage persistence
- `js/map.js` — tile rendering and movement/encounter/portal logic (every level's portal leads straight to Town; Town's leads straight to `currentLevelId`), reading the current zone's cached layout from state
- `js/battle.js` — turn-based combat, player leveling, pet XP gain, chest-loot rolls (gold/item/gear/scroll, scaled by zone depth), and `pickArenaEnemy()` (reskins/scales a random regular-level enemy per Arena wave)
- `js/ui.js`, `js/main.js` — screen wiring and app bootstrap
- `manifest.json`, `service-worker.js`, `icons/` — PWA installability + offline caching
- `scripts/png-lib.js` — shared pure-Node PNG encoder (no image libraries available in this environment)
- `scripts/gen-icons.js`, `scripts/gen-sprites.js` — one-off scripts that generated the app icons, pixel-art sprites, and weapon/armor icons; monster/boss art reuses a handful of body-plan templates (quadruped/biped/flier/blob/serpent, and four boss archetypes) recolored per zone, and gear icons likewise reuse one shared sword/shield template recolored per tier, rather than one-off art for every creature or item

Built to be easy to extend: add a new level by adding an entry to `MAPS` in `data.js` (theme, enemy pool, boss, `nextMap` by zone id — the level's own portal back to Town is automatic) — `mapgen.js` generates its layout automatically, and `map.js`/`ui.js` are already generic across levels. Town, being fixed, is edited directly in `mapgen.js`'s `getTownLayout()`.
